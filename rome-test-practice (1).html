How Rome grew and how the Republic ended
  • Mediterranean control: Rome beat Carthage in the Punic Wars.
  • End of the Republic: Civil wars, a huge gap between rich and poor, and generals with loyal armies. Caesar was killed and Augustus won the power struggle.
  • Republic to Empire: Augustus took control and became the first emperor.
  • Expansion: A conquered city got roads, trade, protection, and Roman law. It also got taxes and lost its freedom.
Leaders
  • Caesar: Popular general who took power. His murder helped end the Republic.
  • Augustus: First emperor. He ended the civil wars and started the Pax Romana.
  • Constantine: Stopped attacks on Christians (Edict of Milan, 313 CE).
  • Pax Romana: About 200 years of peace and good times.
Problems and the fall
  • Fall of the West: 476 CE.
  • Why: Invasions, weak and corrupt emperors, money troubles, and an army that was costly and hard to control.
  • Slave labor: Big farms run by slaves pushed out small farmers. They moved to cities and had no jobs.
  • Praetorian Guard: Protected emperors, but also killed some and sold the throne.
  • Hard to defend: Very long borders and slow communication.
Beliefs and religion
  • Stoics: Live by reason, virtue, and duty. Stay calm and accept what you can't change. All people are equal.
  • King Herod: Ruled Judea for Rome, taxed heavily, and was cruel. Many Jews didn't see him as a true Jewish king.
  • Messiah hopes: Many Jews hoped for a leader who would free them from Rome.
  • Jesus's teachings: Love God and your neighbor, forgive, love your enemies, treat others as you want to be treated.
  • How his story spread: Disciples and apostles, storytelling, the Gospels, and Paul's letters and travels.
  • Why Christianity threatened Rome: Christians wouldn't worship the emperor or Roman gods.
Legacies and America
  • Law: Innocent until proven guilty, equal treatment.
  • Engineering: Roads, aqueducts, arches, concrete.
  • Language: Latin became the base of Spanish, French, and Italian.
  • America: Representative government, Senate, checks and balances, written laws.
